VAT on Shopify fees in FreeAgent: your Shopify bill, app charges and payment fees

7 min readLedgerline

Most UK Shopify sellers notice the same thing: the Shopify bill has no UK VAT on it, so there is nothing to reclaim. That does not always mean the fees can go into FreeAgent with no VAT at all. If the bill is under the reverse charge, HMRC expects you to record VAT on it yourself, even though you pay nothing extra.

This guide is for UK VAT-registered Shopify stores that keep their books in FreeAgent. It covers the two kinds of Shopify fees, how each one reaches your VAT return, and how to enter them in FreeAgent.

Two kinds of Shopify fees

Shopify takes money from you in two different ways, and they need different treatment.

  • Your Shopify bill. This is the bill in Settings > Billing in your Shopify admin. It holds your plan, app charges billed through Shopify, Shopify's own transaction fees (only if you do not use Shopify Payments), and other Shopify charges. It is charged to the payment method on your Shopify account.
  • Shopify Payments processing fees. These are the card fees on each order. Shopify says they are not on your Shopify bill because they are taken out of your payouts.

Why your Shopify bill has no UK VAT

Under Shopify's Terms of Service, stores in Europe, including the UK, contract with Shopify International Limited, a company in Ireland. So your Shopify bill comes from a supplier outside the UK.

When a UK business buys most services from a supplier outside the UK, HMRC applies the reverse charge. The supplier does not add UK VAT. Instead you work out the VAT yourself, at the rate that would apply if you had bought the service in the UK, and put it on your return twice: once as VAT you owe and once as VAT you reclaim. For a business that can reclaim all its VAT, the two cancel out and you pay nothing.

Shopify also asks stores in Europe to give a VAT number or confirm they do not have one in Settings > Billing > Billing profile. Whether VAT is added to your bill can depend on that setting, so look at the bill itself:

  • No VAT on the bill, with a reverse charge note: record it under the reverse charge, as below.
  • UK VAT added to the bill: record it as an ordinary purchase with that VAT, which you reclaim in the normal way if you have a valid VAT invoice.

If you are not sure which you have, download a few bills from Settings > Billing and show them to your accountant.

Which boxes the reverse charge fills

HMRC's VAT Notice 700/12, section 4.6 says the customer of an international service under the reverse charge fills in four boxes:

  • Box 1: the VAT on the service, as if you had charged it.
  • Box 4: the same VAT, as VAT you reclaim.
  • Box 6: the value of the service, without VAT.
  • Box 7: the same value, as a purchase.

So a Shopify plan of £100 a month adds £20 to boxes 1 and 4 and £100 to boxes 6 and 7. Your VAT to pay does not change, but box 6 is higher than your sales alone. That is expected, and it is one reason box 6 will not match your Shopify sales reports exactly. See Shopify sales on your FreeAgent VAT return for the sales side.

If you use the Flat Rate Scheme, Notice 700/12 still tells you to put reverse charge transactions in boxes 1, 4, 6 and 7, outside your flat rate turnover. If part of your business is VAT exempt, the reverse charge VAT may not fully cancel out. Ask your accountant in both cases.

Recording a Shopify bill in FreeAgent

FreeAgent explains how to record services bought from businesses outside the UK. Use one of these methods for each bill, not more than one. FreeAgent warns that recording the same purchase as a bill and as a bank payment counts the cost twice.

Option 1: explain the card payment. When the Shopify charge shows up in your bank or card account in FreeAgent, open it and choose Adjustments > More options. Set the type to Payment, the category to your software or Shopify fees category, VAT Options to Reverse Charge and VAT to Auto. Then click Create New Explanation.

Option 2: add a bill. First edit the Shopify contact in FreeAgent and give it Shopify's address in Ireland, because the Reverse Charge option needs a supplier address outside the UK. Then add the bill and choose Reverse Charge under VAT Options. Once the card payment comes through, explain it as payment of that bill.

FreeAgent only shows the Reverse Charge option on bills dated from 1 January 2021. If your business is in Northern Ireland, FreeAgent uses EC Services instead of Reverse Charge.

Shopify Payments processing fees

Shopify's help page on payout fees says that in the United Kingdom a 0% rate, described as a reverse charge, applies to Shopify Payments fees, and that you keep your own records of the VAT. In practice that means no VAT is taken from you on these fees and there is no VAT to reclaim on them.

How to code them is a judgement call. Card processing can count as an exempt financial service, and accountants differ on whether to self-account the reverse charge on these fees. Either way your VAT to pay is usually the same; only boxes 6 and 7 change. Agree one treatment with your accountant and use it every time.

To record the fees, follow step 4 of matching Shopify payouts and fees with a clearing account: one money out entry per payout in your Shopify account in FreeAgent, using Bank / Finance Charges or your own Shopify fees category, with the VAT treatment your accountant chose.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the bill at "no VAT". If the bill is under the reverse charge, recording it with no VAT leaves boxes 1, 4, 6 and 7 short. Your VAT to pay may still be right, but the return is not.
  • Reclaiming VAT that was never charged. Do not record a Shopify bill at 20% UK VAT unless the bill actually shows UK VAT. A reverse charge bill is not a VAT invoice you can reclaim from.
  • Recording a fee twice. Pick a bill or a bank explanation for each Shopify bill, and record each payout's fees once.
  • Mixing up the two kinds of fees. Your Shopify bill is charged to your payment method. Payment fees come out of your payouts and never reach your bank feed on their own.
