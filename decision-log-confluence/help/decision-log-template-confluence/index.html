How to set up a decision log in Confluence

3 min readDecision Keeper for Confluence

A decision log answers one question months later: what did we decide, who decided, and why. In Confluence you can build one from what is already there, or add an app that keeps the log for you.

1. With what Confluence already has

We read Atlassian's help pages on 26 November 2026. Confluence Cloud gives you three pieces:

  • The DACI template. Create a page from the "DACI: Decision documentation" template. It has a table for status, impact, driver, approver, contributors, informed, due date and outcome, then sections for background and options considered. Atlassian's template page.
  • The Decision element. Type /decision in the editor and pick Decision. It puts one line of text in a grey panel with a green icon, so a decision stands out on the page.
  • The Decision report macro. Insert Decision report on an index page. It lists pages with decisions in the spaces you choose, and you can filter by label or a CQL field. Atlassian's macro page.

This works for one team. The limits show up later. The template is free text, so status and owner are whatever someone typed. Nothing records when the status changed or who changed it. When a decision is replaced, the old page still reads as if it stands, unless someone remembers to edit it.

2. With Decision Keeper for Confluence

Decision Keeper adds a Decision Record macro and a Decision Register for the whole site. Each record has fixed fields, a status and a history.

Install it

  1. A Confluence site admin installs Decision Keeper for Confluence from the Atlassian Marketplace and approves its four permissions. They are read only, plus the app's own storage. The app cannot change your pages.
  2. Open Apps in the Confluence sidebar and choose Decision Register.
  3. The register starts empty. To try it first, choose Add sample decisions. It loads five invented decisions, each marked Sample. Remove sample decisions takes them out again.

Pick a template

Edit the page where the decision is discussed. Type /decision and pick Decision Record. Confluence's own Decision element and Decision report also come up for /decision, so check the name. Publish the page. The macro now shows Record a decision with four buttons, one for each format:

Format Fields it gives you Good for
Architecture Decision Record Author, Reviewers, Context and problem statement, Considered options, Decision outcome and consequences Engineering decisions
DACI Driver, Approver, Contributors and informed, Background, Options considered, Decision and rationale Cross-team business decisions
RFC Author, Approver, Reviewers, Summary and motivation, Alternatives considered, Proposal and rationale Proposals that need comment before sign off
Blank Decision owner, Stakeholders, Context, Options considered, Rationale Anything else

Fill in the record

  1. Give it a Title, a Status and, once it is decided, a Date decided.
  2. Pick the people. The owner field is named for the format (Driver, Author or Decision owner).
  3. Write the context, then the options, one per line, then the decision and why.
  4. Add a Linked Jira issue key if there is one, such as ABC-123.
  5. Choose Save decision. The record shows on the page. To change it later, choose Edit.

Keep it up to date

  • Status. Every record is Draft, Proposed, Decided or Superseded. Each change is kept in the record's history with the person and the date.
  • Replacing a decision. In the new record, pick the old one under Supersedes. The old decision is marked Superseded when you save, and the two records link to each other.
  • Decisions left open. The register shows a banner for decisions that have been open for more than 14 days.

The register is the log

The Decision Register lists the decisions from all spaces, but only those on pages you are allowed to open. You can search it, filter it and choose Export CSV. The next guide shows how: How to find past decisions in Confluence.

What it costs

The Marketplace listing says Decision Keeper is free for up to 10 live decisions. Live means not superseded, and sample decisions do not count. For paid plans, see the Pricing tab on the listing.
