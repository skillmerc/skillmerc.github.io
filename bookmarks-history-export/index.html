Export Bookmarks & History: CSV, Excel, JSON

Export your bookmarks and your browsing history to a file you can open in Excel, Google Sheets or any other tool.

WHAT IT DOES

  • Bookmarks: every saved page with its folder path, title, address and the date it was added. Take all of them, or one folder with the folders inside it.
  • History: the pages you visited, with title, address, visit count and last visit. Choose the last 7, 30 or 90 days, everything, or your own dates.
  • A search box on both lists: type words from a title or an address and only the matching rows stay. The file holds the rows you see.
  • Four formats: CSV (with the marker Excel needs to show accents and non-Latin text), .xlsx, JSON, and for bookmarks the standard HTML file that any browser can import.
  • Long lists are fine: tested with 20,000 visited pages.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN

Everything happens on your computer. The extension makes no network requests at all: no account, no analytics, no ads, no server of ours. The first time you open it, it says what it reads and waits for you to agree. The files it saves are ordinary downloads that only you have.

HOW TO USE IT

  1. Click the extension's button. A new extension's button sits in the browser's Extensions menu (the puzzle piece) until you pin it to the toolbar. A page opens in a new tab.
  2. Pick one of the two lists.
  3. Choose a folder or dates, and type in the search box if you want fewer rows.
  4. Click the format you want to export. The file lands in your downloads folder.

PERMISSIONS, AND WHY

  • Bookmarks. Chrome shows "Read and change your bookmarks". Firefox shows "Read and modify bookmarks". Both are to list them. The browser has one permission for reading and changing; this extension only reads. It never adds, edits or deletes one.
  • History. Chrome shows "Read and change your browsing history on all signed-in devices". Firefox shows "Access browsing history". Both are to list it. This too is the browser's one permission for reading and changing; this extension only reads. It never adds, changes or clears anything. If you sync between devices, the list the browser gives can include pages opened on your other devices.
  • Storage: to remember that you agreed, and which list and dates you used last.

GOOD TO KNOW

  • The list is what your browser has kept. Chrome keeps about 90 days of visited pages, so that is the most any extension can read there. In Firefox, pages you cleared, or that the browser removed to save space, are not in the list.
  • Visited pages come one row per page, not one per visit. Visit count and last visit are the browser's own figures for that page.
  • Dates in CSV and .xlsx files are in your local time. Dates in JSON are ISO 8601 in UTC.
  • A title or folder name that starts with = + - or @ gets a leading apostrophe in CSV files, so a spreadsheet shows it as text and does not run it as a formula. A plain number such as -5 is left as it is.
  • Free. Nothing is locked.
