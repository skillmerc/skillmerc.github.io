WooCommerce and Sage: which integration fits your Sage

3 min readWooCommerce to Sage Accounting

Searching for a "WooCommerce Sage integration" turns up tools that look alike but work with completely different software. Sage sells several separate accounting products, and each has its own way of connecting to other systems. A connector built for one will not work with another. So the first question is not which plugin, but which Sage.

Step 1: find out which Sage you use

  • You sign in through a web browser, and the product is called Sage Accounting (earlier names: Sage Business Cloud Accounting, Sage One). This is Sage's cloud product for small businesses. The UK and Ireland version and the South African version are separate products with separate APIs.
  • The software is installed on a Windows PC or a server. That is usually Sage 50 (small business) or Sage 200 (mid-sized, UK). Your accountant or IT person will know which.
  • Your company runs a larger finance or ERP system from Sage, such as Sage 300, Sage X3 or Sage Intacct. These are mid-market systems and integrations are normally set up as a project with a Sage partner.

Step 2: how each Sage connects to WooCommerce

Your Sage How WooCommerce usually connects Where to look
Sage Accounting (UK and Ireland) A plugin or service that calls Sage's cloud Accounting API WordPress.org plugins, Zapier, and the E-Commerce category of the Sage UK Marketplace
Sage Accounting (South Africa) A different API from the UK version, so UK connectors may not work Check that the tool names the South African product
Sage 50 (desktop) A connector that runs next to the desktop install, or a sync service The "Sage 50" filter on the Sage UK or US Marketplace
Sage 200, Sage 300, Sage X3 Usually a partner-built or partner-installed integration The Sage Marketplace for your country, or your Sage partner
Sage Intacct Its own app marketplace marketplace.intacct.com

The Sage UK Marketplace lets you filter apps by Sage product. For example, it lists a Zapier route for Sage Accounting and WooCommerce, and separate WooCommerce tools for Sage 50.

Step 3: questions to ask any connector before you pay

  1. Which Sage product and country is it built for? "Works with Sage" is not enough.
  2. What does it send, and which way? Orders to Sage as invoices, payments, stock levels back to WooCommerce, or all of these.
  3. Does it keep VAT per line? A single total with VAT lumped together makes your VAT return harder.
  4. What happens with refunds? Some tools create credit notes, some leave that to you.
  5. What if Sage is down or a login token expires? Look for automatic retries that never create a second invoice for the same order.
  6. Where does your data go? Some services pass every order through their own servers; a plugin can talk to Sage directly from your site.
  7. Multi-currency. Many tools only handle orders in your Sage base currency.
